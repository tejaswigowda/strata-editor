FutureFlux Mesa: a planner’s guide to checking walkway shade

This prototype is for participatory planning, co-design and public engagement. It is a digital twin with no live data. No resident has used this tool yet. The rehearsal used a fixed script and synthetic speech; it is not evidence of engagement, endorsement or approval.

1. What does this scene claim?

It compares three samples of land-surface temperature (Encanto, Alta Mesa and Red Mountain) from one Landsat 9 Collection 2 Level-2 acquisition. The Thermal Infrared Sensor 2 observation was recorded on July 29, 2025, at 18:03:53.434497 UTC (11:03:53 in Mesa). A separate geometric check asks whether the proposed tree meshes block direct sun over the center walkway.

2. What does it not claim?

No air temperature, predicted cooling, walkable route, or result for an entire neighborhood. The arrows measure straight-line distance. The 3.3 m paths and 6 m building shell are assumptions, not as-built measurements. The study does not establish that tree cover caused the differences between the three samples.

3. How do I check it?

Open the pinned scene at commit 4612f3d and the STAC item. Use ST_B10 and QA_PIXEL in UTM zone 12N (EPSG:32612). The 17 × 17 product-grid windows start at these zero-based (column, row) offsets: Encanto (6475, 3007), Alta Mesa (6408, 2965), Red Mountain (6541, 3000). Exclude ST_B10 DN 0 and cells with any QA_PIXEL bit 0–5 set. The 30 m grid is not the native thermal resolution; its cells are not independent thermal measurements.

The pinned scene and film are the earlier illustrative version. To reproduce the solar check against that geometry, follow the derived-sun setup on a copy.

4. How do I disagree with it?

Fork the scene into your own repository, change a path or tree location, rerun the shade check, then send the new pinned commit link and the times you checked back to the person who shared this guide. If you have field measurements, identify which assumption they replace. Before sharing a new scene package publicly, confirm layer-level redistribution with Mesa Enterprise GIS and keep the City attribution.

Separate analysis: direct shade on the center walkway

At the exact acquisition instant, 0 of 827 sampled points were in tree shade, both before and after the proposal. A finer 0.2 m grid also returned zero (0 of 3,293). This rules out the illustrative overlay as evidence for shade on this walkway at that instant.

In the July 29 hourly sweep, the existing trees shaded about 9% at 07:00 local time, unchanged by the proposal. All other whole-hour daylight samples from 06:00 through 19:00 returned zero. These snapshots do not establish the result between samples.
